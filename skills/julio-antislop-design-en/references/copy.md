# Copy: the words on the page

Text gives a generated page away before color does: the headline is read first, and a model writes every client in one voice with the same devices. The cause, per Taste Skill, is missing facts; the fix is facts and decisions, never synonyms. A slop word swapped for its synonym is the next tic.

Scope: every rule holds in all modes unless marked **(Persuade)**; §10 says what changes elsewhere. Dated text tells: `tells.md` (Text). Applied at step 8 of `process.md`.

## 1. The fact sheet, before the first sentence

From the brief, `PRODUCT.md`, the existing site and the client's messages, word for word.

| Field | Content | Goes to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | One sentence, no adjective | h1, supporting sentence |
| For whom, and not for whom | The best-fit buyer; who should look elsewhere | h1 or sentence, one FAQ line |
| What they do today instead | A spreadsheet, the rival, nothing | The problem line |
| The real difference | What is true here and not elsewhere, with proof | Claiming headings |
| Hard facts | Price, place, date, count, each with its source | First screen, sentences |
| Customer words | Phrases from reviews, e-mails, tickets | Problem line, headings |
| A limit | What it does not do, who it is wrong for | One admission per page |
| The action | The one transaction and its terms | Buttons, the close |

- **An empty field stays empty**: its section shrinks or goes, and the report lists what the client must supply (A2). Invented specifics (a batch number, an exact minute, an unmeasured percentage) are the current tell.
- **A missing fact the page needs** (a price, the hours): propose a value to the person (`intake.md`, Rhythm). Kept without confirmation, it is sample content under A2: marked in the markup, listed in the report, flagged once on the page. Taste's "assumed" hours and prices on a local business get this treatment, nothing more.
- **A limit or an enemy** (a habit, never the reader) comes from the brief or a plain fact of the trade.
- Check: every number, name and quote traces to a line of the sheet.

## 2. The voice card

Four to six lines in the brief sheet, never on the page: register and reason, person ("you", "we"), usual sentence length, where humour is allowed, the client's house nouns (same word every time), what is never said.

- **The client's own voice first**: a writing sample, the old site or the brief's wording outranks any menu.
- **Otherwise choose a register, with a reason** (buyer, price, the client's real character). Taste's menu, never a default:

| Register | Fits | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facts only | Professional services, B2B, consultants | The facts are thin |
| Technical, dry, with proof | Developer tools, APIs | Spec dump, no pain named |
| Founder's letter | Founder-led products with a stance | The stance is put on |
| Playful, straight-faced | Snacks, drinks, small studios | Jokes hit the buyer, money or health |
| Formal, exact | Skincare, objects, high-end retail | Ornate without being exact |
| Places and restraint | Hotels, heritage, architecture | No real place or history to name |
| Sensory, present tense | Cafés, food, fitness, spas | Every section turns sensory |
| Dated scene | Firms with a real lineage | The history is generic or invented |
| Narrow, by refusal | Makers, specialists | The refusal is invented to sound focused |
| Product as subject | Hardware, one-feature apps | A phrase with no spec under it |
| Hurried, conversational | Freelancers, portfolios | Chummy, information dropped |

- **The model's default voice is out** unless asked: warm, wry, modest, slightly literary, a deadpan closing line, the page calling itself honest or calm.
- **Nine-trades test**: if the card suits nine businesses in other trades, push it toward this client. Log the register in `direction-log.md`; not the same as the last page without a reason.
- **Voice holds, tone drops**: prices, FAQ, forms and errors stay plain. The register never bends §3 to §7.

## 3. The first screen (Persuade)

- **The h1 names the category**, plus the buyer or the place when the brand is unknown to its audience; about 5 words, 32 characters targeted, 8 words and 2 lines at 320 px at most (`persuade.md` §1). Never a riddle, a mood, a slogan, a command or a question. A brand its audience already knows may use its name if the next sentence names the category.
- **The supporting sentence** says what it does, 12 words at most (`persuade.md`; Taste allows 15 to 30, ours wins).
- **One hard fact** from the sheet on the first screen: a price, a place, a date or a count.
- Check: the rival test. Put the closest competitor's name in the h1 and the sentence; if both still read true, rewrite from "The real difference".

## 4. Headings

- **Each heading holds a noun that names a thing**: the product, a part of it, the place, a section noun (prices, rooms, hours, questions) or a sourced figure. 1 to 6 words, 2 lines at most.
- Shapes: the name with a claim under it; a verb and its object; a plain claim with its noun; a sourced figure.
- Not: a bare command, a question (FAQ aside), a colon reveal, an invented capitalised concept, a line clear only after the section.
- Budgets per page: a two-fragment heading "X. Y." once; a number-pair heading ("Four X, one Y") once at most; one figure of speech per page, never the h1, with a fact under it.
- Check: the headings alone, in order, read as this client's table of contents.

## 5. Buttons and links

- **A verb and its object**, 1 to 5 words, saying what happens or what the visitor gets. A bare noun only when the link opens that very thing ("Prices").
- **Never** "Learn more", "Read more", "Discover", "Explore", "Click here", "Submit", nor "Get started" with no object. WCAG 2.4.4 (AA) allows purpose from context; we aim at 2.4.9 (AAA, link text alone): screen-reader link lists read labels out of context.
- **(Persuade)** Two actions at most in the hero, one primary (`persuade.md`). The primary action keeps one wording in the nav, the hero, after the proof and at the close; it appears twice per screen at most. Ten distinct action labels per page at most.
- A form button says what happens and, for an order, how much (`persuade.md` §3 bis). A risk reversal ("free cancellation up to 48 h") sits beside the button only when the sheet gives it.
- One label, one destination, both ways.

## 6. Body text

- Under a heading, 1 or 2 sentences (10 to 40 words); a card body 25 words at most; no paragraph over 5 lines at 1440 px **(Persuade)**.
- **Words per screen (Persuade)**, counted at 1440 × 900 outside interface mocks, code and the legal row: about 35 to 50 for a place, a service or a consumer product; 60 to 80 for a technical product. (Taste judged a fintech page at 117 wordy.) Page total about 900 words outside the footer; the rest goes to sub-pages.
- **A fact where the adjective wants to go**: an adjective that stays is physical or measurable. Show the mechanism in verb-led steps.
- One name for the product, never cycling "the platform, the tool, the solution". Length follows meaning, not a drumbeat of fragments.
- Device budgets per page, from Taste: contrast frames ("X, not Y", "rather than", "instead of", "without the", counted together) 2; negation run ("No X. No Y.") 1; dry closing line 2, never in a row; virtue words about oneself 0; a list of three only when there are three things.

## 7. Facts and numbers

- **One fact, one value, one wording**, repeated word for word where it repeats; never two prices or two opening times for one thing.
- **No number without a source** in the sheet; with its unit, in the sentence it proves, uneven where real. Round unsourced proof figures ("10,000+ teams", "3× faster") are out (A2); a claim needing its source shows it beside it.
- No invented quote, review, rating, logo, client, award or person (A2). Zero testimonials is a normal page.
- **Names**: people only from the brief. A coined brand name is searched with its trade and dropped if taken. Avoid names models keep coining (Taste lists Kestrel, Halcyon, Lumen, Nimbus, Fieldnote, Acme, Northwind; Priya, Okafor, Elara).
- **Contacts** only as the brief gives them.

## 8. Text tells and slop words

The list of text tells is in `tells.md`. `scripts/copy-lint.mjs` carries the word lists in English and French, by family: hype words (seamless, elevate, unlock; *révolutionnaire*, *booster*, *sublimer*), stock phrases ("In today's fast-paced world"; "*Dans un monde en constante évolution*"), virtue words about oneself (honest, calm, simply), leftovers (brackets, lorem ipsum, markdown, chatbot lines) and the page talking about itself ("Below you'll find").

- A word literal for the trade stays (a climbing harness, a seamless knit), and so does the client's own name.
- **Over-correction**: each ban breeds a substitute ("not X but Y" becomes "rather than"; hype words become calm virtue words). Count families together; replace the device with the positive fact.
- Not tells: plain "is", correct grammar, a true list of three, selling on a sales page.

## 9. Check

1. `node scripts/copy-lint.mjs <file> --mode <mode>` (`--allow` for the client's name and literal trade words): zero errors; each warning fixed or justified in one line in the report.
2. Rival test on the h1, the supporting sentence and each heading (§3).
3. **Read aloud**, as the client to a customer; cut what they would not say.
4. Over-correction pass (§8). Then, if several pages were made this session, their h1s and headings side by side: a shared cadence is a tic.
5. A fresh reader given the text only (`critique.md`): what is it, for whom, what it costs, which three lines sound like any site.

The report gives the voice card in one line, every sample value, and what the client must supply.

## 10. What changes per mode

| Mode | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Persuade | Everything above |
| Operate | h1 is the screen or object name ("Invoices"); buttons are strictly verb and object ("Delete invoice"); errors name the field and the fix (`operate.md`); no word budget; voice only in empty states and confirmations, never on a destructive or payment path |
| Read | h1 is the topic or the task in the reader's words; headings may be questions in help and FAQ; long paragraphs allowed within the measure (`typography.md`); "Read more" still out |
| Experience | h1 is the person or studio and what they make; each work gets title, year, role, one or two sentences; the author's voice outranks the menu |
