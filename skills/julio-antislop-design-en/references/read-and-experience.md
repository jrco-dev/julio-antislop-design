# Read and Experience modes

## Read: docs, articles, guides, help, changelog

Structure serves understanding first, then reading comfort.

- Body of 16 to 20 px, line height of 1.5 to 1.7, 60 to 75 characters per line.
- Hierarchical headings with shareable anchors; table of contents visible as soon as the page runs beyond a few screens.
- Links recognizable without color (underlined, with a tuned underline offset); distinct visited states in documentation.
- Code in readable, copyable blocks, with the language indicated; identifiers in monospace within the text.
- Tables with headers, numbers right-aligned in `tabular-nums`.
- Captioned images and diagrams. A diagram replaces a paragraph that describes a structure (a flow, an architecture, a hierarchy).
- Documentation: previous / next navigation, visible last-updated date, search when the volume justifies it.
- Motion: almost none. No GPU effects, no autoplay video.
- Singularity comes through reading typography, the quality of the diagrams and the tone, not through decoration.

## Experience: portfolio, gallery, showcase

The work leads from the first screen; the interface steps back.

- The work takes at least half of the first screen.
- Navigation reduced to the name, 3 to 5 links, one contact.
- One piece in focus per screen; the others are present but recessed (depth, scale, cropping by the edge).
- Each piece has a title, a role, a year and a line of context; full credits.
- Media at their best quality within the performance budget: modern formats, static fallback for every effect, video with a poster frame.
- GPU effects, 3D and rich transitions allowed if they are part of the work and if the A5 floor holds.
- Navigation between pieces by keyboard and by touch, without depending on hover.
- Never an invented work presented as real: a missing piece is a flagged placeholder. If the brief asks for a sample portfolio, each piece is drawn as a finished image of that work (its title card, its poster, its end frame), flagged as a sample.
